What to notice (Kenny's trick ??verify it yourself)
  1. Same x ??Same 罐. The vertical red line in the Cartesian plot lives at \(x = \theta\) (in radians).
  2. Height becomes radius. The \(y\)-value of the cosine wave at that \(x\) is exactly the \(r\) used in the polar plot.
  3. Watch the negative half. When \(y < 0\) (so \(r < 0\)), the polar point flips to the opposite side of the pole. The trace still lands on the same circle ??that's why the circle gets traced completely over \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\).
  4. Cosine vs sine. Switch the function. The circle rotates 90째: \(r = a\cos\theta\) sits on the \(x\)-axis; \(r = a\sin\theta\) sits on the \(y\)-axis. Diameter \(= |a|\) either way.

    TRY: Worked examples ??easy numbers (compute by hand, then check)
    Each example uses a "friendly" angle so \(r\) lands on an integer or half-integer. Click "Jump" to position the red dot on both panels ??your hand-computed answer should match.
    EX 1
    \(r = 2\sin\theta\) at \(\theta = \pi/6\)
    \(r = 2 \cdot \sin(\pi/6) = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} =\) 1
    Cartesian point: \((\pi/6,\ 1)\)
    Polar (x,y): \((\cos\frac{\pi}{6},\ \sin\frac{\pi}{6}) \approx (0.866,\ 0.5)\)
    EX 2
    \(r = 4\cos\theta\) at \(\theta = \pi/3\)
    \(r = 4 \cdot \cos(\pi/3) = 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} =\) 2
    Cartesian point: \((\pi/3,\ 2)\)
    Polar (x,y): \((2\cos\frac{\pi}{3},\ 2\sin\frac{\pi}{3}) = (1,\ \sqrt{3}) \approx (1,\ 1.732)\)
    EX 3
    \(r = 1 + 2\cos\theta\) (lima챌on) at \(\theta = \pi/3\)
    \(r = 1 + 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} =\) 2
    The lima챌on's radius equals \(2\) at this angle.
    EX 4
    \(r = 4\cos(2\theta)\) (rose) at \(\theta = \pi/4\)
