📘 Notes — Photoelectric Effect Toolkit
1. Photon energy
A single photon of light of frequency $f$ (wavelength $\lambda$) carries energy $$E \;=\; h\,f \;=\; \frac{h\,c}{\lambda}.$$
2. Wave relation
$$c \;=\; f\,\lambda \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f = \frac{c}{\lambda},\;\; \lambda = \frac{c}{f}.$$
3. Work function $W_0$
Minimum energy needed to free an electron from a metal surface (a property of the metal). The threshold frequency and wavelength satisfy $$h\,f_0 \;=\; W_0, \qquad \lambda_0 \;=\; \frac{h\,c}{W_0}.$$
4. Einstein's photoelectric equation
For a photon with energy greater than $W_0$, the ejected electron carries the surplus as kinetic energy: $$KE_{\max} \;=\; h\,f \;-\; W_0.$$
5. Stopping potential
The voltage $V_s$ that just stops the fastest electrons: $$e\,V_s \;=\; KE_{\max} \;=\; h\,f - W_0.$$ So $V_s$ (in volts) numerically equals $KE_{\max}$ (in eV).
6. Intensity vs photon count
Power $P$ delivered by a beam $=$ (energy per photon) $\times$ (photons per second): $$N \;=\; \frac{P}{E_{\text{photon}}}, \qquad \text{Intensity } I = \frac{P}{A}.$$
7. Diffraction grating
For a grating with $d$ = distance between adjacent slits, $$d\,\sin\theta_m \;=\; m\,\lambda \quad (m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots).$$ Distance on a screen at $L$: $\;y_m = L\,\tan\theta_m.$
Useful constants and conversions:
$h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\!\cdot\!\text{s}$  ·  $c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$  ·  $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$
$1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$  ·  $h\,c \approx 1.988 \times 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\!\cdot\!\text{m} \approx 1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm}$
Worked Examples
Example 1 — Photon energy from wavelength
A laser pointer emits red light at $\lambda = 650\,$nm. What is the energy of one photon? Give the answer in both joules and electron-volts.

Idea. Use $E = hc/\lambda$. The shortcut $hc \approx 1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm}$ makes the eV form trivial.

Step 1 — In joules. $E = \dfrac{(6.626\!\times\!10^{-34})(3.00\!\times\!10^{8})}{650\!\times\!10^{-9}} = \dfrac{1.988\!\times\!10^{-25}}{6.50\!\times\!10^{-7}} = 3.06\!\times\!10^{-19}\,\text{J}.$

Step 2 — In eV. $E = \dfrac{1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm}}{650\,\text{nm}} = 1.91\,\text{eV}.$

Answer. $E \approx 3.06 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J} \approx 1.91\text{ eV}$

Example 2 — Threshold + Stopping potential
Sodium has a work function $W_0 = 2.28\,$eV. Light of $\lambda = 400\,$nm strikes a sodium surface. (a) Find the threshold wavelength. (b) Find the stopping potential $V_s.$

(a) Threshold wavelength. $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{hc}{W_0} = \dfrac{1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm}}{2.28\,\text{eV}} \approx 544\,\text{nm}.$ Light with $\lambda > 544\,$nm cannot eject electrons from Na.

(b) Photon energy at 400 nm. $E = 1240 / 400 = 3.10\,\text{eV}.$

Stopping potential. $eV_s = E - W_0 = 3.10 - 2.28 = 0.82\,\text{eV}$ $\;\Rightarrow\; V_s = 0.82\,\text{V}.$

Answers. $\lambda_0 \approx 544$ nm, $V_s \approx 0.82$ V

Problems 7 – 15
Problem 7 Visible-light frequency & photon-energy range
Statement
According to Wikipedia, the human eye is sensitive to light with wavelengths between $380\,$nm and $750\,$nm.
  1. What range of frequencies of radiation does this correspond to?
  2. What range of photon energies does this correspond to?
Solution
(a) Frequencies. Use $f = c/\lambda$.
$\;\;f_{\text{at }750\text{nm}} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{7.50 \times 10^{-7}} = 4.00 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$
$\;\;f_{\text{at }380\text{nm}} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{3.80 \times 10^{-7}} = 7.89 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$
$f \in [4.00\times10^{14},\; 7.89\times10^{14}]$ Hz
(b) Photon energies. $E = hc/\lambda = 1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm}/\lambda$:
$\;\;E_{750} = 1240/750 = 1.65\,\text{eV} \approx 2.65 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
$\;\;E_{380} = 1240/380 = 3.26\,\text{eV} \approx 5.23 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
$E \in [1.65,\;3.26]$ eV $\approx [2.65,\;5.23]\!\times\!10^{-19}$ J
Mnemonic. Red end = lower frequency, lower energy. Violet end = higher frequency, higher energy.
Problem 8 Work function $W_0 = 4.8 \times 10^{-19}\,$J
Statement
A photoemissive surface has a work function of $4.8 \times 10^{-19}\,$J.
  1. What is the minimum photon energy needed to cause electrons to be emitted from the surface?
  2. Determine the threshold frequency and wavelength for photoemission from this surface.
Solution
(a) Min photon energy. Equal to the work function: $E_{\min} = W_0$
$E_{\min} = 4.8\times 10^{-19}$ J $\approx 3.00$ eV
(b) Threshold.
$\;\;f_0 = W_0/h = (4.8\times10^{-19})/(6.626\times10^{-34}) = 7.24\times10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$
$\;\;\lambda_0 = c/f_0 = (3.0\times10^8)/(7.24\times10^{14}) = 4.14\times10^{-7}\,\text{m}$
$f_0 \approx 7.24 \times 10^{14}$ Hz, $\lambda_0 \approx 414$ nm
Sanity check. 414 nm is violet light — makes sense, this metal needs energetic photons.
Problem 9 Work function $W_0 = 3.0\,$eV
Statement
A photoemissive surface has a work function of $3.0\,$eV.
  1. Determine the threshold frequency and wavelength for this material.
  2. Photons cause electrons to be emitted with a stopping voltage of $2.0\,$V. Determine the energy of the photons and the wavelength of the light.
Solution
(a) Threshold. $\lambda_0 = hc/W_0 = 1240\,\text{eV}\!\cdot\!\text{nm} / 3.0\,\text{eV} = 413\,\text{nm}.$ $f_0 = c/\lambda_0 = 3.0\!\times\!10^8/4.13\!\times\!10^{-7} = 7.25\!\times\!10^{14}\,\text{Hz}.$
$f_0 \approx 7.25 \times 10^{14}$ Hz, $\lambda_0 \approx 413$ nm
(b) With $V_s = 2.0$ V. $KE_{\max} = eV_s = 2.0\,$eV. So $E_{\text{photon}} = KE_{\max} + W_0 = 2.0 + 3.0 = 5.0\,\text{eV} = 8.01 \times 10^{-19}\,$J.
$\lambda = 1240/5.0 = 248\,\text{nm}.$
$E = 5.0$ eV ($8.01\times10^{-19}$ J), $\lambda \approx 248$ nm
Problem 10 He-Ne laser, $\lambda = 633\,$nm, $P = 0.50\,$W
Statement
A He-Ne laser has a wavelength of $633\,$nm and an output power of $0.50\,$W.
  1. What is the photon energy for this light?
  2. The laser hits a black target. How many photons hit the target per second?
Solution
(a) Photon energy. $E = hc/\lambda = 1240/633 = 1.96\,\text{eV} \approx 3.14 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}.$
$E \approx 1.96$ eV ($3.14\times10^{-19}$ J)
(b) Photons per second. $N = P/E = 0.50/3.14\!\times\!10^{-19}.$
$N \approx 1.59 \times 10^{18}$ photons/s
Insight. Even a small 0.5 W laser emits about $10^{18}$ photons every second — light is "lumpy" at this scale, but the lumps are so numerous it looks continuous.
Problem 11 $\lambda = 440\,$nm grating with $10{,}000\,$lines/cm
Statement
Light of wavelength $440\,$nm is incident on a grating with $10{,}000\,$lines/cm.
  1. What is the angular deflection of the first and second order maxima?
  2. What are the positions of these maxima on a screen that is $1.2\,$m from the grating?
  3. Suppose it is desired to detect the maxima with a photocell. What is the maximum allowable work function of the cell if it is to respond to the light?
Solution
(a) Angles. Slit spacing $d = 1/(10{,}000\,\text{cm}^{-1}) = 1.0\times10^{-6}\,\text{m}.$ Use $d\sin\theta_m = m\lambda$:
$\;\;\sin\theta_1 = 440\!\times\!10^{-9}/1.0\!\times\!10^{-6} = 0.44 \Rightarrow \theta_1 \approx 26.1^\circ.$
$\;\;\sin\theta_2 = 0.88 \Rightarrow \theta_2 \approx 61.6^\circ.$
$\theta_1 \approx 26.1^\circ,\;\; \theta_2 \approx 61.6^\circ$
(b) Positions at $L = 1.2$ m. $y_m = L\tan\theta_m:$
$\;\;y_1 = 1.2\,\tan(26.1^\circ) = 1.2 \times 0.490 = 0.588\,\text{m}.$
$\;\;y_2 = 1.2\,\tan(61.6^\circ) = 1.2 \times 1.847 = 2.22\,\text{m}.$
$y_1 \approx 0.59$ m, $y_2 \approx 2.22$ m
(c) Max work function. For the photocell to fire, $W_0 \leq E_{\text{photon}} = hc/\lambda = 1240/440 = 2.82\,\text{eV}.$
$W_0 \leq 2.82$ eV ($\approx 4.52 \times 10^{-19}$ J)
Problem 12 Silver — threshold $\lambda_0 = 262\,$nm
Statement
The threshold wavelength for the photoelectric effect for silver is $262\,$nm.
  1. Find the work function for silver.
  2. Find the stopping potential if the incident radiation has a wavelength of $200\,$nm.
Solution
(a) Work function. $W_0 = hc/\lambda_0 = 1240/262 = 4.73\,\text{eV} \approx 7.59 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}.$
$W_0 \approx 4.73$ eV ($7.59\times10^{-19}$ J)
(b) Stopping potential @ $\lambda = 200$ nm. $E_{\text{photon}} = 1240/200 = 6.20\,\text{eV}.$ $eV_s = E - W_0 = 6.20 - 4.73 = 1.47\,\text{eV}.$
$V_s \approx 1.47$ V
Problem 13 $\lambda = 400\,$nm beam, intensity $100\,$W/m²
Statement
A light beam of wavelength $400\,$nm has an intensity of $100\,$W/m$^2$.
  1. What is the energy of each photon in the beam?
  2. How much energy strikes an area of $1.0\,$cm$^2$ perpendicular to the beam in $1.0\,$s?
  3. How many photons strike this area in $1.0\,$s?
Solution
(a) Photon energy. $E = 1240/400 = 3.10\,\text{eV} \approx 4.97 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}.$
$E \approx 3.10$ eV ($4.97\times10^{-19}$ J)
(b) Energy on $1$ cm² in $1$ s. $1\,\text{cm}^2 = 1\times10^{-4}\,\text{m}^2.$ $U = I\,A\,t = 100 \times 1\!\times\!10^{-4} \times 1.0 = 1.00 \times 10^{-2}\,\text{J} = 10\,\text{mJ}.$
$U = 0.010$ J
(c) Photons/sec. $N = U/E = 1.00\!\times\!10^{-2}\,/\,4.97\!\times\!10^{-19}.$
$N \approx 2.01 \times 10^{16}$ photons
Problem 14 Cesium — $W = 1.9\,$eV
Statement
The work function for cesium is $1.9\,$eV.
  1. Find the threshold frequency and wavelength for the photoelectric effect.
  2. Find the stopping potential if the wavelength of incident light is $300\,$nm.
Solution
(a) Threshold. $\lambda_0 = 1240/1.9 \approx 653\,\text{nm}.$ $f_0 = c/\lambda_0 = 3.0\!\times\!10^8 / 6.53\!\times\!10^{-7} \approx 4.59 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}.$
$f_0 \approx 4.59 \times 10^{14}$ Hz, $\lambda_0 \approx 653$ nm
(b) Stopping potential @ $\lambda = 300$ nm. $E = 1240/300 = 4.13\,\text{eV}.$ $eV_s = E - W = 4.13 - 1.9 = 2.23\,\text{eV}.$
$V_s \approx 2.23$ V
Why cesium? Very low work function → fires on visible red/orange light. Used in old TV photodetectors and photoelectric cells.
Problem 15 $60\,$W bulb, $2.1\%$ efficient, $\lambda_{\text{avg}} = 550\,$nm
Statement
A $60.0\,$W light bulb is $2.1\%$ efficient ($2.1\%$ of the electrical energy is converted into visible light) and the average wavelength of the light is $550\,$nm.
  1. What is the average energy of a visible photon produced by the light bulb?
  2. How many visible photons does the light bulb emit per second?
Solution
(a) Photon energy. $E = 1240/550 = 2.25\,\text{eV} \approx 3.61 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}.$
$E \approx 2.25$ eV ($3.61\times10^{-19}$ J)
(b) Photons/sec. Visible power $= 60.0 \times 0.021 = 1.26\,\text{W}.$ $N = P_{\text{vis}}/E = 1.26 / 3.61\!\times\!10^{-19}.$
$N \approx 3.49 \times 10^{18}$ photons/s
Comparison. The 60 W bulb spits out about twice as many visible photons per second as the 0.5 W laser of Problem 10 — but the bulb's energy goes mostly into infrared (heat), which is why "$2.1\%$ efficient" hurts so much.
