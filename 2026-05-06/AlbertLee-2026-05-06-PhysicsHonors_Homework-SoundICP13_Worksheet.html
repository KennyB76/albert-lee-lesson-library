EQUATION SHEET
Reference page — keep this open while working. All formulas you need for the 14 problems.
1 · Speed of Sound in Air
  • $v_{\text{sound}} = 331 + 0.6\,T$   ($T$ in °C, $v$ in m/s)
  • At 20 °C: $v \approx 343$ m/s
2 · Wave Equation (any wave)
  • $v = f\,\lambda$
  • Period: $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$
  • Light: $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s
3 · Intensity & Decibels
  • $I = \dfrac{P}{4\pi r^{2}}$  (point source)
  • $\beta = 10\,\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)$ dB
  • $I_0 = 1.0 \times 10^{-12}$ W/m²
4 · Wave on a Stretched String
  • $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}\,$,  $\mu = \dfrac{m}{L}$
  • $T$ = tension (N), $\mu$ = linear mass density (kg/m)
5 · String Fixed at Both Ends
  • $\lambda_n = \dfrac{2L}{n}$,   $f_n = \dfrac{n\,v}{2L}$
  • $n = 1,2,3,\dots$ (all integers)
6 · Pipe Open at Both Ends
  • $\lambda_n = \dfrac{2L}{n}$,   $f_n = \dfrac{n\,v}{2L}$
  • $n = 1,2,3,\dots$   (same as string)
7 · Pipe Closed at One End
  • $\lambda_n = \dfrac{4L}{n}$,   $f_n = \dfrac{n\,v}{4L}$
  • $n = 1,3,5,\dots$   (odd only)
8 · Pipe Closed at Both Ends
  • $\lambda_n = \dfrac{2L}{n}$,   $f_n = \dfrac{n\,v}{2L}$
  • Same harmonics as open-open (different pressure pattern)
9 · Free-Fall (problem 13d)
  • $h = \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t_{\text{fall}}^{2}$
  • $g = 9.8$ m/s²
10 · Resonance Tube (problem 14)
  • Closed end at water; resonances at $L = \dfrac{(2n-1)\,\lambda}{4}$
  • Successive lengths differ by $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$
★ Quick Decision Guide
  • Both ends free (open-open pipe, string fixed-fixed) → all $n$, $f_n = nv/2L$
  • One end closed, one end free → odd $n$ only, $f_n = nv/4L$
  • Both ends closed (problem 12c) → all $n$, $f_n = nv/2L$  (but pressure pattern differs)
  • "First overtone" = next harmonic above $f_1$  (open: $f_2$, closed-one-end: $f_3$)

  1. What is the intensity of the sound at a distance of 2.5 m?

6. The velocity of waves traveling along a stretched string is: $$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{m/L}}$$ where $T$ = tension (N), $m$ = mass of string (kg), $L$ = length (m).

    1. Determine the velocity of wave propagation along the string.
    2. Determine the frequency of the third harmonic of the string.

    1. Determine the wavelengths of the first two harmonic waves in the rope.
    2. Determine the frequencies of the first two harmonic waves in the rope.
